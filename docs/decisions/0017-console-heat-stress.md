- **Date raised:** 2026-11-15 (David Chousal Cantu)
- **Owner:** CS lead (David Chousal Cantu)
- **Implements:** [SCO-314](https://linear.app/scout1/issue/SCO-314)
- **Builds on:** [Telemetry Methodology §3](../analysis/telemetry-methodology.md) (the CRW method
  in `analytics/telemetry/bleaching.py`), [ADR-0012](0012-authenticated-console.md) (roles,

> **Summary** — The console Dashboard now shows NOAA Coral Reef Watch heat stress for each buoy:
> the daily mean of its trusted readings, HotSpot, Degree Heating Weeks over 84 days and the CRW
> alert level, computed in the Worker by a port of `bleaching.py` that is tested against the
> Python's own output. The MMM each buoy needs is **looked up from CRW's 5 km product for the
> buoy's position** (David's choice, 2026-11-15), and can be typed in by hand instead. The same
> numbers reach `/api/v1`, a new "bleaching alert at or above" email rule and Fred.

---

## Context

The thesis and the team expect the console Dashboard to show heat stress; it showed water
temperature only. The method already exists and is settled: `analytics/telemetry/bleaching.py`
implements CRW's HotSpot, DHW and the eight-level alert scale (Liu et al. 2014; Skirving et al.
2020; Levels 3–5 adopted 2026-10-23, SCO-212). DHW is undefined without a site **MMM** (Maximum
Monthly Mean), a long-baseline climatology that a short buoy record cannot supply. The Python
takes it as `--mmm`; the console had nowhere to keep one.

## Decision

1. **The MMM is per buoy, in D1** (migration 0017: `latitude`, `longitude`, `mmm_c`,
   `mmm_source`, the CRW cell it came from, who set it and when). Members and admins set it on
   Settings → Buoys; guests see it read-only; every change is audited as `buoy.mmm`. A buoy with
   no MMM shows no heat stress and says how to set it, never zeros.
2. **Default source: NOAA CRW's 5 km product at the buoy's position.** The Worker asks NOAA's
   ERDDAP dataset `NOAA_DHW` (coastwatch.pfeg.noaa.gov, which serves it from PacIOOS) for
   `CRW_SST` and `CRW_HOTSPOT` on the newest day at the nearest 0.05° cell, and stores
   **MMM = CRW_SST − CRW_HOTSPOT**. CRW defines HotSpot as SST − MMM and keeps negative values
   (valid range −15…15 °C), so this recovers CRW's own MMM for that cell. Checked 2026-11-15 at
   Kāneʻohe Bay (cell 21.475° N, 157.775° W): 26.87 °C on 2026-02-15, 2026-08-15 and 2026-11-10
   alike. A land, ice or no-data cell returns nothing, and the console says so. **Manual entry**
   stays available (e.g. from the site's CRW virtual station) and overrides the lookup.
3. **The computation runs in the Worker** (`console/worker/heat-stress.js`), a line-for-line port
   of `bleaching.py` and the daily mean in `aggregate.py`. A shared fixture generated by the
   Python itself (`analytics/telemetry/tests/fixtures/heat-stress-cases.json`) is checked against
   both: by `test_heat_stress_fixture.py` on the Python side and `heat-stress-test.js` on the
   console side, within 1e-9. Trusted readings are those the Python trusts: no `RTC_LOST` flag,
   a timestamp from 2020 on, and a temperature in −5…45 °C.
4. **Exposed four ways:** a heat-stress Dashboard bay (in the layout editor and the built-in
   Overview), `GET /api/v1/heat-stress` and `/api/v1/buoys/{id}/heat-stress` under
   `read:telemetry`, a fourth email-rule kind `bleaching` (fires when the newest daily mean's
   level reaches the rule's level; re-arms one level below, so a Warning ↔ Alert Level 1 swing
   does not flap; ADR-0015's opt-in, caps and off links unchanged), and Fred's `get_heat_stress`.

## Deviations from the Python, stated plainly

- **Coverage floor.** The Python gives a day a mean only if at least half of its samples are
  present, counting against 48 (the SD log's 30-minute cadence). The console sees only the 1–3
  rows a day the buoy transmits, so the same 50 % floor counts against the buoy's reported
  transmit count (3 by default): two of three readings, or one of one. Same rule, different
  denominator. A daily mean of two or three instantaneous readings is noisier than a mean of 48.
- **"Current" is the newest day with a daily mean.** The Python's series ends at its last input
  day; so does the console's, and it shows that day's date. Email alerts ignore a newest day
  more than one day old, so a silent buoy does not keep alerting on old heat.
- **The 12-week trend window.** The bay and the API show the last 84 days; each day's DHW still
  looks back its own 84 days, so 168 days of readings are read.

## Contradictions found, surfaced not reconciled (standing rule 1)

- **A day with no data is labelled "No Stress" by `bleaching.py`** (`alert = No Stress if
  hs_today is None`). The methodology doc does not say this. It reads as "measured and fine"
  when it means "not measured". The port keeps the Python's behaviour so the two agree; the
  console never shows such a day as the current state, but its series and table do. Worth
  changing in both, together.
- **The [Liu 2014 reading note](../hub/research/notes/liu-2014-dhw.md) says to source the MMM from
  the CRW *virtual station***; this ADR uses the 5 km *cell* at the buoy's position. A virtual
  station averages several reef-containing pixels around a site, a cell is one pixel; nearshore
  they can differ. Manual entry covers the virtual-station route.
- **[`sources.md`](../hub/research/sources.md) row `noaa-crw-baa-2023` still says "the pipeline
  still stops at Level 2"**; the pipeline has computed Levels 3–5 since 2026-10-23 (SCO-212).
  Corrected in the same PR.

## Alternatives considered

- **Manual entry only** (the default had David not chosen): no outbound call, matches
  `run_telemetry.py --mmm`, but every buoy needs someone to find its MMM by hand. Kept as the
  override.
- **Compute in Python and push results to D1:** reuses the reference directly, but the console
  has no Python runtime, and a separate job would add a moving part and a lag.
- **Read the MMM from CRW's climatology NetCDF** ([NCEI Accession 0185742](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/archive/accession/0185742), CoralTemp v1.0, 1985–2012; `noaa-crw-clim-2019` in [sources.md](../hub/research/sources.md)): the authoritative file, but ~100 MB and not
  queryable per point from a Worker. The ERDDAP subtraction gives the same number per cell.

## Consequences

- The console depends on NOAA's ERDDAP being up **only when someone presses Look up**; heat
  stress itself is computed from D1 and needs nothing external.
- Absolute DHW stays indicative: the buoy's in-water daily mean runs warm against CRW's
  night-only satellite SST (methodology §3 caveats). The bay says so.
- A new email-rule kind meant rebuilding `alert_rules` (SQLite cannot widen a CHECK); the
  migration keeps `alert_state` and `alert_off_links` and was checked to keep their rows and
  cascades, on Node's SQLite and on wrangler's local D1.
