# Margaritis et al. 2025 — Satellite vs in-situ SST on Caribbean reefs

> Reading note · part of the [Knowledge Hub](../../README.md) library.
> Source row: [`margaritis-2025`](../sources.md#in-situ-temperature-reference-data) · DOI [10.1371/journal.pclm.0000480](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pclm.0000480) · 🔓 open access (PLOS Climate)

**What it says.** Compares two satellite SST products against in-situ reef loggers in the Caribbean.
**CoralTemp — the product behind NOAA CRW's DHW — overestimated the SST warming trend by as much as
0.20 °C per decade**, while a reprocessed analysis product (ESA CCI) aligned better with actual reef
temperatures, *especially in coastal areas and during temperature extremes* — i.e. exactly when and where
bleaching risk is decided.

**Why it matters to S.C.O.U.T.** This is arguably the single strongest external justification for S.C.O.U.T.'s core
value proposition. It shows, in a peer-reviewed reef context, that the satellite product degrades precisely
in the nearshore, high-stakes regime — so an affordable in-situ logger at the reef is not redundant with
satellites, it *corrects* them. It also cautions our own analysis: if S.C.O.U.T. ever compares its record to
CoralTemp, expect a warm-trend bias in the satellite, not an error in the buoy.

**Caveat / how to use it.** Caribbean sites, two specific products — the magnitude won't transfer verbatim
to Hawaii, but the *direction and mechanism* (land contamination, skin/diurnal effects nearshore) are
general. Use it to frame S.C.O.U.T. as ground truth and to pick the right satellite product for any
cross-comparison, not as a fixed correction factor.
