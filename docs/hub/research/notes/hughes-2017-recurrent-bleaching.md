# Hughes 2017 — Global warming and recurrent mass bleaching of corals

> Reading note · part of the [Knowledge Hub](../../README.md) library.
> Source row: [`hughes-2017`](../sources.md#thermal-stress--coral-bleaching-dhw) ·
> DOI [10.1038/nature21707](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature21707) · 🔒 copyrighted (*Nature*);
> free author manuscript at [hdl.handle.net/10072/342667](http://hdl.handle.net/10072/342667)

**What it says** (checked against the PubMed abstract, PMID 28300113, 2026-10-26). Record
temperatures in 2015–2016 triggered a pan-tropical bleaching episode, the third global-scale event
since mass bleaching was first documented in the 1980s. Using aerial and underwater surveys of
Australian reefs with satellite sea-surface temperature, the authors find:

- The footprint of bleaching on the Great Barrier Reef in 1998, 2002 and 2016 followed that year's
  pattern of sea temperature.
- Water quality and fishing pressure had minimal effect on the 2016 bleaching, so local protection
  gives little or no resistance to extreme heat.
- Past bleaching in 1998 and 2002 did not lessen the severity of bleaching in 2016.

**Why it matters to S.C.O.U.T.** It backs thesis Chapter 1's point that heat-driven mass bleaching
is a recurring global threat, and that sea temperature is the signal that explains where it
strikes, which is why the buoy measures it in the water.

**Caveat / how to use it.** The abstract does **not** say that bleaching now returns faster than
reefs can recover. An earlier draft of Chapter 1 said so and was corrected on 2026-10-26. Find
and check a source for that claim, and add it to the registry, before making it again. The no-resistance finding is from the Great Barrier Reef, not a global result.
