# Eddy 2021 — Global decline in coral reef ecosystem services

> Reading note · part of the [Knowledge Hub](../../README.md) library.
> Source row: [`eddy-2021`](../sources.md#thermal-stress--coral-bleaching-dhw) ·
> DOI [10.1016/j.oneear.2021.08.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oneear.2021.08.016) ·
> 🔓 open access, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 (*One Earth*, Cell Press)

**What it says** (checked against the abstract, 2026-10-26). A global assessment of how much reefs
can still provide to people, built from reef habitat extent, reef fishery catch and effort,
Indigenous consumption of reef fish, and reef-associated biodiversity. Its headline numbers:

- Global coverage of living coral has declined by half since the 1950s.
- Catches of reef-associated fish peaked in 2002 and are falling despite rising fishing effort;
  catch per unit effort is down 60% since 1950.
- At least 63% of reef-associated biodiversity has declined with the loss of coral.

**Why it matters to S.C.O.U.T.** It is the citation for the opening claim of thesis Chapter 1, that
reefs are in steep decline worldwide.

**Caveat / how to use it.** The abstract does **not** say that the capacity to provide ecosystem
services has itself halved; only coral cover halved. An earlier draft of Chapter 1 said
"capacity … has fallen by about half" and was corrected on 2026-10-26. Cite the coral-cover and
fisheries figures, not a single "half of all services" figure.
