## Where the project stands

The first in-lab weekly of the quarter, with Navid present, and the most productive meeting of
the project so far for closing things rather than opening them: **two long-open decisions
closed, one purchase removed from the BOM, and a staged order abandoned in favour of a single
one.**

The substantive engineering change is the **base-station MCU**. Navid brought a finding from
another lab project: the Feather M0 is Arduino-based and therefore single-threaded, so it
cannot service LoRa and WiFi concurrently — it goes deaf during blocking calls. The base station
moves to a **Feather S3 (ESP32, FreeRTOS)** with a separate LoRa module, which is also cheaper
precisely because the radio is not integrated. **The buoys do not change** — a sense-log-transmit
cycle with no concurrent link has no use for threads. This closes
[SCO-86](https://linear.app/scout1/issue/SCO-86) after a month open, and it is the first place
the buoy and shore station deliberately run different hardware.

The second closure is smaller but had been sitting since 2026-08-17: **"Fred" stays.** The
objection was always the namesake rather than the name, and the resolution was to drop the
namesake — the assistant is named after nobody and needs no dedication line. Worth recording
because the issue had drifted into a research task ("find a better scout to name it after") when
the actual answer was that the premise was wrong.

**Permitting turned the expensive way.** Dr. Bartley confirmed the team handles its own
permitting for the Hawaii site, so [SCO-99](https://linear.app/scout1/issue/SCO-99)'s hoped-for
outcome — joining an existing authorization — is gone, and the DAR, OCCL and USACE tracks are
all live with months of lead time.
