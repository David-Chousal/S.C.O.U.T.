**Phase:** 1 — Subsystem Bring-Up (2026-09-07 – 2026-10-16)

## Where the project stands

The first full-buoy assembly exists in Fusion. For the first time, every current part is in one
model, together with the mooring bracket and a candidate cable route. The bracket's mounting
method is now decided.

## Changed today

- **Mooring bracket mounting: through-bolts, nuts and an internal backing plate.** This replaces
  the heat-set-insert attachment modeled in the FEA. Through-bolts carry the ~810 N LC9 snap,
  which could pull inserts out of PETG. The backing plate spreads the nut load and closes the
  backing-plate gap flagged in the August design panel review. Each penetration is sealed with a
  bonded sealing washer or marine sealant, with epoxy as a secondary seal only. The bolt pattern
  follows the `3076T33` stand-in (4 × Ø1/4 in holes on a 1.250 × 1.125 in rectangle). The
  backing plate is 316 stainless, 2.50 × 2.25 × 0.125 in. Details in [facts.md](../facts.md).
- **Bracket attached and full buoy assembled in Fusion** from the updated parts.
- **Candidate cable routing modeled:** from the top-cap port, over the rim, down the outside of
  the float, under the bottom, then down the line to the sensor string. This is a concept only.
  See the [render](../../assets/diagrams/buoy-assembly-cable-routing-2026-09-21.png).
- **Hawaii site permitting:** John emailed Dr. Hannah Barkley (NOAA) to ask about test access
  at the buoy site she mentioned and whether an existing permit covers it. SCO-99 is closed, and
  the reply is tracked on SCO-113.

## Still open

- Chassis-bottom thickness at the bolt location, which sets the bolt length. It's about 4 in if
  the bolts pass through the full 76 mm section.
- The final 316 pad-eye part, which may change the bolt pattern, and an FEA re-run on the
  through-bolted interface (SCO-69, SCO-75).
- How the cable route handles abrasion, snagging, clamping and clearance from the mooring line.
