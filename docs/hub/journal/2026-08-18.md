## Where the project stands

Mechanical design (John Ryan, `geng`) closed the biofouling mitigation decision and made
progress on floatation: a physical fit-test print, a weight datapoint, and a pivot in what the
structural design is being validated against.

## Changed today

- **Biofouling mitigation decided** ([SCO-15](https://linear.app/scout1/issue/SCO-15)):
  antifouling coating (over copper mesh and mechanical wipers), specific product **Sea Hawk
  Smart Solution** (copper-free, Econea biocide) — chosen over copper-based general-retailer
  options, which conflict with the reef-safe principle set for the mooring
  ([ADR-0004](../../decisions/0004-reef-safe-anchoring-and-mooring.md)).
- **Bolted-wedge cross-section fit test printed** — a single row of the bolt pattern (not a
  full wedge) sliced to confirm the bolted fit against the chassis without spending filament on
  a full-height piece. Fit confirmed. Slicer weight estimate: **~300 g per full-size wedge**,
  now logged in [`facts.md`](../facts.md#mechanical--deployment) as the input the
  [SCO-48](https://linear.app/scout1/issue/SCO-48) buoyancy check needs.
- **Floatation validation target pivots to impact survivability.** The 2026-08-17 FEA pass (min
  safety factor 25.4 against a provisional SF≥4 check) is now read as over-engineered relative
  to the project's cost target. Future testing targets impact/boat-strike survivability at
  controlled cost instead — FEA impact/drop-load simulation plus bench impact tests on printed
  samples, tracked as [SCO-71](https://linear.app/scout1/issue/SCO-71). Full narrative:
  [`mechanical/cad/floatation/README.md`](../../../mechanical/cad/floatation/README.md#cross-section-fit-test-print-and-a-validation-target-pivot--2026-08-18).
- **New open question: propeller-resistant mooring/tether line**
  ([SCO-72](https://linear.app/scout1/issue/SCO-72)) — a boat crossing the line should ideally
  foul the propeller rather than cut the line and lose the buoy adrift. ADR-0004 already scoped
  final line material/diameter as a routine sizing decision; this adds a concrete criterion to
  weigh against it.
