- **Electronics housing reprint failed too.** After the 2026-11-12 upside-down misprint
  ([journal](2026-11-12.md)), the reprint was printed with bed adhesion lowered on purpose, so the

## Changed 2026-11-13 — mechanical (John, [SCO-309](https://linear.app/scout1/issue/SCO-309))

- **Test Assembly 10 roll-decay CFD finished** (30 degree start, 6 s, fins meshed at 1.25 mm, run overnight on the
  Mac in two sittings). Period 0.824 s with fins and 0.822 s without, against 0.78 s from Capytaine. Fins case swing
  falls from 46.7 to 27.6 degrees (about 1.2 percent of critical damping); the no-fins case swing grows from 50.9 to
  63.9 degrees, which is numerical energy gain, so the fins-versus-no-fins gap (about 2 percent of critical at best)
  is not a clean measurement. Decision: keep the fins; the tub drop test with and without fins, from 10 and 30
  degrees, is still the reference. Results, plots and snapshots are in the
  [hull-hydrodynamics README](../../../mechanical/simulations/hull-hydrodynamics/README.md#test-assembly-10-multi-night-run-run-2026-11-12-to-2026-11-13).

## Changed 2026-11-13 — website (David)

- **Technology page stability explorer tidied up (Phase 1 of a three-phase revamp).** One timeline of design chips
  replaces the slider, the Earlier/Later buttons and the jump-to dropdown. The step list and instructions that said
  the same thing three times are cut to one line. The boxed cards are now one flat surface split by hairlines, and the
  stability side is wider (5:7) so the chart is easier to read. The verdict comes first, then the six readouts in an
  even grid, then the chart. Design notes moved into "How this design was modelled". The payload slider is hidden
  where the design fixes the mass. The explorer and the embedded 3D viewer are now light only, matching the site;
  before, a reader in dark mode saw a dark viewer next to the light stability panel. Arrow keys inside the stability
  panel no longer change its design out of step with the picker. No physics or results changed.
- **Phase 2 (not started):** one native component instead of three nested iframes, the 3D model floating in water at
  its computed draft, and the righting-arm curve linked to the model's tilt.
- **Phase 2 of the revamp: the stability explorer is one native stage, no iframes.** Each design floats in 3D
  at the draft the stability simulator computes. Tilting it (slider, or dragging along the righting-lever curve)
  turns the hull about its waterline centre and draws weight down through G, buoyancy up through B, and the lever
  GZ between them. "Let go" turns it the way the curve says until it comes to rest (direction only, not to time
  scale). The page now fetches only the model of the design in view (0.05 to 1.2 MB) instead of 8.1 MB up front,
  and three.js loads only when the stage nears the screen. Numbers are the simulator's own, read from its generated
  page by `analytics/telemetry/site/design_explorer.py`. The old iframe pages (`static/design-explorer/`,
  `static/stability/`, `static/viewer/`) and the standalone viewer's `?embed=1` mode are removed.
- **Phase 3: compare, assemble, "How we know".** Pin one design's righting-lever curve and pick another: the
  pinned curve stays dashed under the live one, its chip is marked, and a side-by-side table lines up draft,
  freeboard, GM, strongest push and tip-over angle (each at the payload it was pinned with). The parts come
  together once, the first time the stage is half on screen (skipped under reduced motion), and "Spread parts"
  pulls them apart on demand. The method paragraph under the stage is now three numbers: 3 mm cubes, 5° steps,
  about 1% against a plain cylinder.
