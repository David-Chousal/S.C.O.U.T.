**Phase:** 0 — Kickoff (2026-08-14 – 2026-09-20)

## Where the project stands

John Ryan iterated the flotation wedge to a **thin-wall v5** — 5.5-in height
([SCO-110](https://linear.app/scout1/issue/SCO-110)), outer curved wall 0.095 in, radial side
walls 0.063 in — chasing print mass and material cost. The thin open walls flexed during
printing and broke first-layer bed adhesion; the fix was an **internal bracing web with
lightening cutouts**. A closed-form structural check was run against the wedge-relevant load
cases. Nothing else moved.

## Changed today

- **Flotation wedge v5 (thin walls + DFM bracing web)** — full narrative in
  [`design-notes.md`](design-notes.md) and
  [`mechanical/cad/floatation/README.md`](../../mechanical/cad/floatation/README.md#thin-wall--dfm-bracing-web-iteration-v5--2026-09-07).
  - Walls thinned over several slicing passes to **outer 0.095 in / sides 0.063 in**, both now
    fully-dense perimeter shells (no infill core) — a change from v4's "3–4 walls + 15 %
    gyroid."
  - **Internal bracing web** added to connect the previously-open walls. **DFM fix and
    structural fix in one change** — it stops the print-bed flex *and* halves every wall
    panel's free span *and* adds a third shear web to the wedge-to-chassis load path.
- **Wall-thickness structural check** —
  [`mechanical/test/wedge-wall-thickness-structural-check-2026-09-07.md`](../../mechanical/test/wedge-wall-thickness-structural-check-2026-09-07.md).
  Closed-form hand calcs against LC3–LC5 (wave/current), LC8 (50.3 kPa hydrostatic), plus
  foam-fill expansion and print/handling flex.
  - **Outer wall 0.095 in — accepted as the floor.** SF 7.3 on yield under LC8; buckling
    suppressed foam-backed. Do not thin further.
  - **Side walls 0.063 in — marginal. Specified up to 0.080 in (2.00 mm).** SF ≈ 2 on the
    foam-fill manufacturing transient and single-wedge shear buckling; 2.00 mm → SF ≥ 3 for
    ~+2 % wedge mass.
  - **Internal bracing web is mandatory**, not optional.

## The consequence that matters

**The foam fill is reclassified from "buoyancy redundancy" to "required structural backing."**
At the v4 0.250-in wall the printed shell was self-supporting and the foam was a bonus
(cracked-wedge-keeps-buoyancy). At 0.095 in the outer wall, bare, is marginal on
external-pressure buckling — it needs the foam. Practical effects:

- Foam must be in **before any submersion or pressure test** — "assemble, test, then foam" is
  not a valid sequence.
- Foam fill + shell adhesion becomes a **structural sign-off item**, not just a process step.
- A *cracked but foam-filled* wedge still keeps its buoyancy (unchanged — closed-cell foam
  doesn't flood); a *foam-degraded or unfoamed* wedge is now a structural concern too.
