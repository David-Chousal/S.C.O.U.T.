| 2026-08-18 | mechanical | Biofouling mitigation | Chose the coating family (over copper mesh and mechanical wipers), then researched four specific product candidates against cost, low-quantity availability, and general-retailer convenience. Found a real tension: the only general-retailer (Home Depot) options are copper-based, which conflicts with the project's reef-safe principle established for mooring ([ADR-0004](../decisions/0004-reef-safe-anchoring-and-mooring.md)) — copper is toxic to coral at low concentrations. Copper-free alternatives (Econea, zinc pyrithione biocides) exist but only from specialty marine suppliers, at 2–4× the cost. Presented both paths without picking one — this is a call for the mechanical lead, not a default | [Biofouling Antifouling Coatings](../research/biofouling-antifouling-coatings.md) |
| 2026-08-17 | mechanical | Floatation (wedge design) | **Final family chosen: the bolted wedge variant**, over the snap/keyhole Master V3 from the 2026-08-15 entry below and over Outer Octagon. This morning's print test tried to integrate new bottom caps into the snap/keyhole body, reusing its previously-working keyhole slide-in function — it broke under the added complexity, reinforcing the move to the simpler bolted + heat-set-insert assembly. New in this pass: a bottom cap under each wedge (gyroid infill, many wall layers) for impact protection. Foam fill and the surfboard-stringer-inspired radial webs between wedges carry over from the snap/keyhole design. First FEA (Autodesk Fusion, static 300 N side load, ABS material properties) gives a minimum safety factor of 25.4 against an SF≥4 check used only as this study's pass/fail bar — not yet a validated target, since max expected loads haven't been derived yet. Print material (PETG vs ABS/ASA) remains separately open. This entry also corrects the same-day SCOUT Weekly meeting summary in [decision-log.md](decision-log.md), which predates the bottom caps and stated the SF figure without the provisional-target caveat | [mechanical/cad/floatation/README.md#bolted-variant-chosen--2026-08-17](../../mechanical/cad/floatation/README.md#bolted-variant-chosen--2026-08-17), [mechanical/test/fea-floatation-side-load-2026-08-17.md](../../mechanical/test/fea-floatation-side-load-2026-08-17.md) |
| 2026-08-17 | mechanical | Electronics/sensor housing (O-rings) | Settled the open question from the 2026-08-15 entry below: purchased off-the-shelf standard O-rings chosen over both the TPU-printed experiment and a printed-injection-mold batch-cast option. Printed parts are porous along layer lines — a waterproofing-critical risk at the 5 m pressure target — and standard rings have well-characterized long-duration compression-set behavior that an untested printed part doesn't. The mold-tooling investment for batch-casting also doesn't pay back at capstone quantities. Provisional: revisit if standard AS568 sizes don't fit the housing tolerances | [SCO-55](https://linear.app/scout1/issue/SCO-55), [facts.md](facts.md#mechanical--deployment) |

The turbidity polarity hint started in the panel's unit slot, passed every test, and was only caught by building the page and looking: it wrapped the panel head onto two lines. Moved to the legend, which has room and sits next to the series anyway. Lesson: string assertions cannot see layout — a browser pass belongs in the loop for anything touching panel heads, cards, or legends. | [docs/engineering/live-dashboard.md](../engineering/live-dashboard.md) |
