# Monterey and Santa Cruz Dock Test — Who to Ask and What It Takes

> **Summary** — Who to contact at Santa Cruz, Monterey and Moss Landing to hang the S.C.O.U.T. test
> buoy from a dock for about a week as a **systems test, not data collection**, and every permit,
> permission and piece of our own work that sits around that request. The short answer: **the dock
> owner's permission is the real gate**; federal and state agencies are most likely light for a
> small, attended, unanchored, discharge-free buoy, but none has said so in writing yet. Written
> answers are the goal, not guesses.
>
> **Not legal advice.** A student team's reading of public pages and search summaries, done on

> [§0](#0-how-to-read-this-document)). Confirm with the named office before relying on it.

## 1. What we are asking for

Every host will ask the same questions. Settle the answers once, then reuse them.

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| What it is | S.C.O.U.T. test buoy: 3D-printed PETG body, about 1100 g dry, sealed electronics |
| Purpose | Floating, seals, waves, fouling, corrosion, battery life; sensors read and a packet reaches shore ([SCO-182](https://linear.app/scout1/issue/SCO-182)). **No scientific data is collected for reef health** |
| Duration | About one week, possibly extended to 2 to 4 weeks |
| Where | Floating beside a dock, hung or tethered by a short line to a cleat or rail. **No anchor, nothing on the seabed** |
| Attended | Checked daily, removed on request or in bad weather |
| Discharge | None. No sampling, no chemicals released |
| Battery | Sealed lithium primary cells (Li-SOCl₂ D cells, [ADR-0007](../decisions/0007-primary-cell-power-source.md)). **Disclose this to every host** |
| Radio | 915 MHz LoRa at +14 dBm, low power ([FCC review](fcc-915-mhz-compliance.md)) |
| Insurance | SCU coverage is the team's to confirm; see [§5.1](#51-before-we-ask-anyone-our-side) |
| Vessel? | No. It is a small floating instrument, not a boat. Say so, because harbor rules are written for vessels |

### 2.1 Notes on each host

**Santa Cruz Harbor.** Guest slips are first come, first served with no reservations. The
posted limit is two weeks, extendable by the harbormaster, and checkout is noon. Registration is
in person with a vessel registration number. Insurance is requested for short stays and required
to keep a slip license (a 2018 ordinance, revised 2020). The pages say **nothing about objects
attached to docks, research gear, or fees for non-vessel use**. ✅ for the above; ❓ for the
rest. Because the whole framework is built around vessels, the cleanest path is a written
exception from the harbormaster. Do not assume the vessel rules apply, or that they do not.

**Monterey Harbor.** Every vessel needs a berth or mooring assignment from the Harbor Office.
Transient berths are first come, first served, with payment by Dockwa or phone. The page says
nothing about attached objects or research gear. ✅ The municipal code section for harbor
rules could not be loaded (a 404 on the URL tried), so the code is unread. ❓

**Moss Landing Harbor District.** Its page lists permit types that require a CEQA review deposit:
construction, rental business, **short-term facilities use, and special activities**. Whether a
tethered buoy counts is not stated. ✅ The fee schedule (revised July 1, 2026) and the berth
checklist exist as documents; the checklist could not be read. ❓

**Yacht clubs.** Neither club's own site could be read. Both are private, so the answer depends on
a member or the board. A club is the right call only if it owns the dock; a club on a city-owned
dock still needs the city's OK. ❓

## 3. The permission layers around the dock

Ordered from the one that decides everything to the ones least likely to apply.

| Layer | Applies? | What we know | Action | Mark |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dock owner or operator** (port district, city, club, university) | **Yes, always** | Pages describe vessel rules only | Get a **written yes**: dates, line attachment point, who approves, who to call | ✅ |
| **Harbor ordinance or code** | Likely, for any object left in a harbor | Not found for either harbor | Ask the harbormaster whether an unmanned instrument is a vessel, an obstruction or exempt | ❓ |
| **Insurance and indemnity** | Likely asked | Santa Cruz requires insurance to keep a slip license. A host may want a certificate | Ask SCU Risk Management for a certificate naming the host as additional insured | ⚠️ |
| **Monterey Bay National Marine Sanctuary** | Probably not, for a tethered buoy | Permit coordinator Kacy Cooper (2026-10-16): tethered monitoring buoys **likely need no sanctuary permit**. Rule text: [15 CFR 922.132(a)(4)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/15/part-922/subpart-M) prohibits placing a structure on submerged lands; its listed exceptions do not name a tethered floating buoy, so the answer rests on the coordinator, not the text. She also said harbor areas outside sanctuary jurisdiction may work | Name the dock in the reply and ask for the answer in writing. Application route if needed: email kacy.cooper@noaa.gov, subject "New Permit Application" | ✅ rule text, ⚠️ email and phone |
| **US Army Corps of Engineers, Section 10** | Unclear | [33 CFR 322.3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/33/322.3) requires a permit for structures or work in navigable waters. Its definition of "structure" lists a permanent mooring structure and a permanently moored floating vessel. A temporary buoy tied to an existing dock is not obviously either. Another district's permit exempts temporary recreational buoys removed within 30 days, but that is not this district's rule | Ask the San Francisco District Regulatory Division whether a temporary, tethered, attended instrument at an existing dock needs authorization. Contact page could not be loaded; an old Carmel-area list names a branch chief at 1455 Market St, San Francisco, (415) 503-6773 (2012, stale) | ⚠️ |
| **US Coast Guard, private aids to navigation** | Probably not, but the buoy is a floating object | [33 CFR Part 66](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/33/part-66) covers private aids, applied for with form CG-2554 to the District Commander. A research buoy tied inside a marina is not marking a channel, so this is likely not an aid | Ask in the same email whether it needs marking or notice. Keep it clear of channels and fairways | ⚠️ |
| **California Coastal Commission or local coastal permit** | Unknown | Coastal Act waivers exist for minor projects. No result addressed temporary floating objects at a dock | Ask the Central Coast District office: 725 Front St, Suite 300, Santa Cruz CA 95060 · (831) 427-4863. Also ask the city or port district whether it holds the coastal permit for the dock | ⚠️ |
| **State Lands Commission** | Probably only the landlord's concern | Harbor districts and cities usually hold the tidelands lease. Not confirmed here | Ask the harbormaster if the lease limits what can attach | ❓ |
| **Marine mammals** (federal and state protections) | A practical risk | Sea lions rest on marina docks. A loose line is an entanglement risk, and approaching or disturbing animals is regulated | Short rigid tether, no slack line, daily check, stay away from animals | ⚠️ |
| **FCC** | Handled | 915 MHz at +14 dBm; compliance fix adopted ([FCC review](fcc-915-mhz-compliance.md)) | Confirm the bench build uses the adopted configuration | ✅ |

## 4. Our own work before and during the test

### 4.1 Before we ask anyone (our side)

1. **Advisor approval for off-campus work.** The SCU
   [Student Project Hazard Assessment](https://www.scu.edu/media/school-of-engineering/pdfs/senior-design/Dry-Lab-Project-Hazard-Assessment-Form-2025.pdf)
   requires offsite locations to be written in the form and approved by the faculty advisor.
   Add the chosen dock, supervision plan and emergency procedure to the team's form.
2. **Insurance.** Ask SCU Risk Management what coverage applies to a student project in a
   public harbor and whether a certificate can name the host.
3. **Lithium cell disclosure.** Tell the host in writing: sealed Li-SOCl₂ cells, no charging.
4. **One-page description with a photo** of the buoy, the tether point and the contact sheet.

### 4.2 Hardware and procedure

| Item | What to settle |
|---|---|
| Tether | Short, rigid or semi-rigid, at a cleat or rail the host names. No slack line. Chafe guard |
| Tide and surge | Range at the chosen dock; line must follow tide without hanging the buoy or letting it slam the dock |
| Wake and traffic | Place away from the fairway and transient berths; boat wakes load the tether |
| Contact tag | Weatherproof tag on the buoy: "Research test, SCU S.C.O.U.T.", dates, name and phone |
| Retrieval | A stated way to remove it in under 30 min on request |
| Daily check | Who, when, what to look for (fouling, damage, battery, line wear) |
| Theft and vandalism | Cable lock option, or accept the loss; photograph before and after |
| Fouling and corrosion | Take before and after photos and weights; this is part of the test |
| People | Never work alone on a dock; life jacket; follow the host's dock rules |
| Antenna | Never transmit without an antenna attached |
