# Monterey Bay National Marine Sanctuary Permitting

> **Summary** — What the Monterey Bay National Marine Sanctuary (MBNMS) permit coordinator told John Ryan
> on 2026-10-16 about deploying S.C.O.U.T. for a nearby test, and what is still unanswered. This is an
> agency reply by email, not a written determination, and it is the Monterey counterpart to
> [Hawaii Deployment Permitting](hawaii-deployment-permitting.md). Linear: [SCO-191](https://linear.app/scout1/issue/SCO-191),
> [SCO-183](https://linear.app/scout1/issue/SCO-183).

---

## What MBNMS said (Kacy Cooper, permit coordinator, 2026-10-16)

| Topic | Answer |
|---|---|
| Discharge | Prohibited in the sanctuary: [15 CFR 922.132(a)(2)(i)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-15/section-922.132) |
| Altering submerged lands | Prohibited: 15 CFR 922.132(a)(4) |
| Research that puts equipment on the seafloor (such as research mooring anchors) or in the water column | Needs a permit from the Office of National Marine Sanctuaries |
| Tethered ROVs or monitoring buoys | **Likely no sanctuary permit** |
| Anchoring the buoy to the seafloor | Fill out a sanctuary application form and send it to her |
| Short-term deployment | Areas within and outside the harbors that are outside sanctuary jurisdiction may work |

## Her question back to us (unanswered)

What existing structure would the buoy be tethered to? The answer decides whether a permit is needed, so it
should be settled before replying. Consistent with
[ADR-0004](../decisions/0004-reef-safe-anchoring-and-mooring.md), tethering to existing infrastructure avoids a
seafloor anchor.

## Open items

- Decide whether a tether-only test at Monterey is the plan, or an anchored one (which needs the application).
- Confirm the harbor areas outside sanctuary jurisdiction if a short-term test is wanted.
